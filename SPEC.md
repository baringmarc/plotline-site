## 1. Overview

Marketing site for a court booking product. Venue owners read about it, see the demo and
reviews, and send a contact form; we then set their venue up with them.

### Goals
- Convert venue owners: clear pitch, pricing, and a short contact form.
- Show social proof: venues and approved reviews.
- Bring open-play organizers to the site with Paddle Stack (`/paddle-stack`, Milestone 5), a free open-play queue that leads them to the venue pitch. Rules: §4 "Paddle Stack rules". Since Milestone 6, a stack can go live: players follow it on their own phones, view-only (§4 "Live stacks"), and a Plotline venue's open-play session hands its stack off with the venue's logo.
- Keep abuse low: validation shared by browser and server, honeypot, timing check, CAPTCHA before launch.

### Non-goals (v1)
- Booking itself (that is the court booking product), payments, an owner dashboard.
- Player accounts or records, and players changing a live stack: Milestone 6's live stacks are view-only for players (§4 "Live stacks").

**Page patterns:**
- Each section opens with a small uppercase label, an `h2` and a short lead, left-aligned, with no accent bar.
- Plain, tinted and dark sections take turns. Cards, ✓/✕ lists and stat tiles use the existing colour tokens and work in both themes.
- The CTA to the contact form (`#contact`) appears in the nav, the hero, after "What it costs" and in the final band.
- Sections may fade in on scroll. That is off under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and the fade-in never hides content (the page itself needs JavaScript, §6 2026-11-02).
- No live counters (venues, players, bookings) until they read real numbers; never show a zero.

**Pricing claims** (must match SAAS-PLAN §1; the amount isn't final, so it is one value in config):
- Free for venues: no subscription, no setup fee, nothing per hour.
- A ₱10 booking fee per confirmed online booking, paid by the player, shown at checkout and collected by the venue with the court price.
- Players pay the venue directly. Plotline never holds venue money and bills the fees on one monthly invoice.
- Not billed: open play, blackouts, and bookings the venue cancels (the player gets the fee back).
- The site doesn't mention PayMongo (T27.9, 2026-11-15): venues pay into their own GCash, Maya or bank and approve a proof.
- Never claim: a free trial, self-serve sign-up, weekly billing, an owner-pays-the-fee switch, or walk-ins as a feature (they're being hidden).

**Feature claims:** only what court-booking-system ships. Its SAAS-PLAN Phase 5 "Copy" lists the gaps, and none of them may be claimed until they ship:
- open play sign-ups, slots, a waitlist or skill levels (open play is a marked session on the grid, with details and up to 3 links such as an RSVP form or Reclub);
- reminders;
- paying at the venue;
- a venue logo on the booking page.

## 4. Flows

### Owner contact (T1.7)
1. The form at `#contact` (`features/contact`) checks the fields with `src/lib/siteForms.ts`, an exact copy of the platform's rules. It needs the consent box (linked to `/privacy`) and, when a Turnstile site key is set, a passed check.
2. It posts the PLATFORM.md §4 fields to the platform's `site-contact` function, with the honeypot, the time of first touch and the Turnstile token. The function stores a lead; no account is created.
3. `200 { ok: true }` shows "Thanks, we'll be in touch", plus a "book a call" link when `site.bookCallUrl` is set. Any other reply shows the function's own message, and the Turnstile widget resets for a fresh token (each token works once).
4. Site key: `VITE_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY`. Without it, development uses Cloudflare's always-pass test key, and a production build shows no widget (the function then refuses the form).

### Reviews
The review form posts to the platform's `site-review` function, which stores the review with `approved = false`. Only approved reviews are public (read from `reviews`).

### Paddle Stack rules (Milestone 5, T5.2; Milestone 8, T8.3, T8.4)
A free open-play queue for organizers (`/paddle-stack`). The engine (`src/features/stack/engine.ts`) is pure: every action takes a session and returns a new one, never mutating its input; an action that breaks a rule throws a `StackError` with a `code` and leaves nothing changed. It works on one device and stores nothing itself.

**Session.** An optional title (trimmed, at most 60 characters, `bad-title` above that; shown on the board and the TV and used as the standings image's heading), a format, 1–12 courts, players and games. **Play** is singles (2 players per court, one a side) or doubles (4, two a side), set for the whole session. **Teams** is an open queue (the board makes the teams) or fixed pairs (doubles only). Each player has a status: *waiting* (in the line), *playing*, *out* (time out) or *removed*, and an optional **skill level** 1–3 (none fits any group; `setLevel` sets or clears it, anything else is `bad-level`). Names are trimmed; an empty name or one already in the session (ignoring case) is skipped. Players added later join the back of the line.

**Formats.** Seven: Fair rotation, Winners stay & split, Americano, Round Robin, Mexicano, Skill groups, Winners vs Losers. **Round formats** (Americano, Mexicano) start a new round only when no game is on any court; the others are **continuous** and fill each free court as soon as they can (`isRoundFormat`).

**Combinations** (refused with `bad-combo`): fixed pairs aren't offered in singles (any format); Winners stay & split isn't offered in singles; Americano and Mexicano (changing partners) aren't offered with fixed pairs. Round Robin, Skill groups and Winners vs Losers work in singles, open doubles and fixed pairs.

**Sides.** A *side* is what queues as one: a player, or a fixed pair (its partners move as one). Round Robin schedules sides; Skill groups and Winners vs Losers pick sides.

**Fixed pairs.** Players are entered as pairs (`createSession` with `pairs`, `addPairs` later; `addPlayers` is refused with `needs-pair`). A pair with an empty or taken name, or the same name twice, is skipped whole. Partners always stand next to each other in the line and move as one: the first two pairs in line play pair 1 against pair 2; a time out, *Back in line* or *Remove* on either partner applies to both. Removing a pair that is on court cancels the game and the other pair goes to the front of the line.

**Players.**
- *Time out:* only a waiting player. They leave the line and keep their record. *Back in line* puts them at the back.
- *Remove:* takes a player out of the line and the standings; their finished games stay. If they are on court, that game is cancelled with no score. The other players in that game go back to the **front** of the line in their order (in a round format they simply wait for the next round).

**Filling courts** (`fillCourts`). Continuous formats fill each free court, lowest court first, while enough players are waiting (4 in doubles, 2 in singles) and the format's rule finds a game (Round Robin may leave a court free, below). Round formats start a new round only when no game is on any court.

**Results.** A game ends with a score (whole numbers 0–99 per side, no tie) or is skipped (no winner, no score; it still counts as a turn played). Only scored games count in the standings.

**Fair rotation.** Doubles: the 4 waiting longest go on: line positions 1 & 3 against 2 & 4 (with fixed pairs, the first pair against the second). Singles: the 2 waiting longest play each other. When the game ends, everyone in it goes to the back of the line in the order they came on (with fixed pairs, pair by pair).

**Winners stay, split up.** The first game on a court is taken like Fair rotation. After a scored game, the losers go to the back of the line, and the winners stay but split up: the first winner partners the first player in line, the second winner the second (winner 1 + next 1 against winner 2 + next 2). A player who has won 2 games in a row on that court goes off after that win: all four go to the back, losers first, and the court is refilled from the front of the line. After a skipped game, all four go to the back in the order they came on. Doubles only. With fixed pairs the winners can't split: the winning pair stays together and plays the next pair in line, with the same cap of 2 wins in a row.

**Americano.** Rounds: each round uses `min(courts, floor(available / per court))` courts (4 in doubles, 2 in singles), where available means waiting or playing (not out or removed). Who plays: fewest turns played first, then line order. Those who sit out keep their place. Pairing: for that round's players, use partners who haven't partnered before; a repeat partner is used only when no pairing of that round's players avoids it (searched exactly for up to 24 players a round, within a step limit so a phone never hangs; best effort above). Then teams are matched on courts to keep repeat opponents low. In singles there are no partners: the round's players are paired as opponents, avoiding repeat opponents the same way. The round ends when every court has a result or a skip. Everyone keeps their own wins, losses and points.

**Round Robin** (continuous; a fixed schedule). The schedule is every pairing of two sides, in circle-method order (`roundRobinOrder`): the sides in the order they joined (with an empty slot when the count is odd), the first fixed and the rest rotating one place each round, so each round meets every side once. In singles and with fixed pairs a pairing is a match: those two sides play each other. In open doubles a pairing is a *partnership*: everyone partners everyone once, and a game is two partnerships with no player in common (the first is team A). The schedule is built over the sides that are waiting or playing, so a late arrival, a time out, *Back in line* or a removal rebuilds the rest of it; finished games (scored or skipped) still count. A pairing's *count* is how many finished games it has been in (as a match, or as a partnership), and the current cycle is the lowest count over the schedule. A free court (lowest first) takes the first pairing in schedule order with that lowest count whose players are all waiting; when none is waiting, the court stays free until a game ends. In open doubles it then adds the partnership that, of those with no player in common whose players are all waiting, has the lowest count, then comes first in schedule order (so a court never stays empty for want of a second team); if there is none, the court stays free. Once every pairing has been played, the next cycle starts. After a game everyone goes to the back of the line in the order they came on. The line only shows who's waiting: games don't come from its front.

**Mexicano** (rounds; singles and open doubles). Who plays in a round is chosen like Americano: `min(courts, floor(available / per court))` courts, fewest turns played first, then line order; those who sit out keep their place. Those chosen are then ordered: in the first round (no game scored yet in the session) by skill level, 3 first, a player with no level counting as 2, then line order; after that by their place in the live standings, then line order. Taken four at a time in that order, each group plays 1 & 4 against 2 & 3 on the next court (lowest first); in singles, two at a time, 1 against 2. After a game everyone goes to the back of the line in the order they came on.

**Skill groups** (continuous). Level 3 is the strongest. A fixed pair's level is the higher of its partners' (none if neither has one). A free court (lowest first) builds a group from the side waiting longest: walking the line from the front, it takes each side whose level matches the group's, or that has no level, until the court is full; the group's level is the first level taken (sides with no level fit any group). If the line can't fill a group that way, the court takes the sides at the front of the line, whatever their level, so the side waiting longest always goes on the next free court and nobody waits forever. The group's players keep their line order on court: 1 & 3 against 2 & 4 (fixed pairs: pair against pair; singles: 1 against 2). After a game everyone goes to the back of the line in the order they came on.

**Winners vs Losers** (continuous). Each side's *pool* is the result of its last scored game: winners or losers; a side with no scored game yet (new, or only skipped games) fits either pool. A free court builds a group exactly as Skill groups does, with the pool in place of the level: from the side waiting longest, it takes sides from the same pool or with none, and if the pool is short of players it takes the sides at the front of the line (the longest waiting from both pools). Teams and the line work as in Skill groups.

**Standings** (`standings`). Players not removed, ranked by wins, then win rate, then point difference, then points scored. Players equal on all four share a place. Each row shows wins, losses, games, points for and against.

**Saving.** `serialize` turns a session into JSON with a `version` (2); `deserialize` returns `null` for anything malformed or with an unknown version, and never guesses. A version 1 save (before T8.3) loads as doubles with an open queue and no title, so a stack saved before the update resumes.

### Live stacks (Milestone 6, T6.1; contract: court-booking-system `docs/PLATFORM.md` §4 "Live stacks")

**Snapshot** (`features/stack/live/snapshot.ts`): the organizer's device builds it from the session and publishes it; the player view shows only what it holds. `v: 1`; `title`; `play`; `format`; `courts`; `players` (`id`, `name`, `status`; no levels, no partner links); `line` (waiting player ids, front first); `games` (`id`, `court`, `teamA` and `teamB` as player ids, `status`, `scoreA` and `scoreB` once scored). Nothing secret goes in it. At most 64 KB as UTF-8 JSON (`snapshotFits`). `readSnapshot` gives nothing for an unknown `v`, a size over the cap or any bad shape, and never guesses.

Standings aren't stored: the player view works them out from `players` and `games` with the engine's `standings()`, which reads only those fields.

**API** (`features/stack/live/api.ts`): `goLive` calls `stack-live` through `lib/functions.ts` (Turnstile token, courts, title). `getLiveStack` and `publishLiveStack` call the RPCs with the publishable key only. Failures are a `LiveError` with a stable code: the RPC's hint, or the function's error code; anything else is `unknown`. Words for the organizer come with T6.2.

**Organizer** (T6.2, `features/stack/live/`: `GoLivePanel.tsx`, `useLiveStack.ts`, `publisher.ts`, `liveKey.ts`): on the board, **Go live** asks for a fresh human check (Turnstile; a check that expires or fails says so, and nothing is sent), then calls `stack-live` with the courts and the title. The share link `/paddle-stack/live/<code>` and a QR (`qr-creator`, drawn on a canvas) appear under the button, with a copy button and a status line: Live, Reconnecting… or Stopped with the reason. Every change publishes the board, at most once every 2 s (changes inside the gap collapse into one publish of the latest). `too_fast` retries the latest board after the gap with the same version; `stale_version` re-reads the stack and continues from its version; `ended`, `forbidden` (another device took over), `not_found`, `limit_reached`, `too_large` and `invalid_snapshot` stop publishing with a plain message; a network failure shows Reconnecting and retries every 5 s. **End session** publishes the final board with `end: true`. **Start over** forgets the live key. While this device holds a live key and publishing hasn't stopped for good, the organizer's page joins its own channel `live-stack:<code>` and keeps it (T6.9, PLATFORM.md §4 Realtime: today's message partition then exists before the first publish); it ignores what arrives, keeps the channel through Reconnecting, and leaves it on Start over, a permanent stop, a new code (Go live again or a hand-off) and leaving the page. The `?tv=1` window, which never publishes, joins none. `unavailable` says Go live is off and the board keeps working. The live key (code, secret, last version) stays in this browser's `localStorage` (`plotline.paddle-stack.live`), next to the session; the secret goes only to `publish_live_stack`. A stack with a key loads on the board and publishes at the next change. Live stacks never change the start screen or the share cards; the TV board shows the join QR while live (T6.6): in TV mode while this device has a live key and publishing hasn't stopped, and in the `?tv=1` window while a live key is saved on this device (that window doesn't publish, so it follows the key through `storage` events, not the status).

**Hand-off from a venue** (T6.3, `live/handoff.ts`, `StackPage`): `/paddle-stack?live=<code>#key=<secret>` (made by a venue admin's `/admin/open-play`). The page takes the code and the secret, removes the query and the fragment from the address bar at once (whatever happens next), and reads the stack. A link that doesn't parse, or a code that is unknown or expired, says so and stores nothing; a stack that can't be read says so too. A valid link stores the key with the venue's name and logo path, and starts a session with the stack's title and courts (format fair, no players yet); that start counts as the first change, so it publishes once, as the stack's version + 1. A session already running on this device asks first (Replace it or Keep my session; Keep changes nothing); an ended one is replaced without asking. The board then shows "Open play at <venue>" and the venue's logo from the public `venue-media` bucket, and that header comes back after a reload from the stored key.

**Player view** (T6.4, `live/viewer.ts`, `useLiveViewer.ts`, `PlayerView.tsx`, `subscribeLiveStack` in `api.ts`): `/paddle-stack/live/<code>` reads the stack once (`get_live_stack`) and joins the public channel `live-stack:<code>` (event `snapshot`; a broadcast that isn't a valid snapshot is dropped). While the channel is `CLOSED`, `CHANNEL_ERROR` or `TIMED_OUT` it reads the stack every 15 s until it reconnects, then reads once more to catch up; a board whose version isn't above the one shown is ignored. It shows a status (Live, Reconnecting… or This session has ended) and "Last updated" with the time the shown board arrived, in the phone's own time zone. The board is rebuilt from the snapshot (`sessionFromSnapshot`): the title (or "Live stack"), each court with its players and win streaks, Up next, on a time out, and the standings worked out on the phone; no controls. A venue's stack shows "Open play at <venue>", its logo and **Book a court** (`https://<slug>.plotline.fit/book`), also once ended. Ended shows the final standings only. Loading says so; an unknown or expired code (also when a poll finds it gone) says "This stack has ended"; a failed first read says so with Reload; a failed poll keeps the board. A stack with no board yet says the board appears when the organizer starts it. **Share my card** (T6.5, `live/ShareMyCard.tsx`): under the standings (live and final), enabled once a game is scored; it opens a "Your name" picker (the players in the standings, by name, after a "Pick your name" prompt) with Make my card and Cancel; Make my card opens the T8.7 player card's preview (`ShareCardDialog`, the same sizes, backgrounds, photo and layout) for that player, drawn on the phone from the shown board, so it redraws when the board changes and stays open if the stack ends meanwhile; closing it puts focus back on Share my card; nothing is fetched or sent. Under it, a link to Paddle Stack. An untitled stack reads back with `title: null`, taken as no title.

**Checks:** `npm run test:live` runs `e2e/live/smoke.spec.ts` against court-booking-system's local stack (`LIVE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` from `npx supabase status`): go live, publish, read back, and the `too_fast`, `stale_version` and end paths. The device matrix doesn't run it. `e2e/live/player.spec.ts` (T6.4) has the organizer publish in one browser context while a phone context follows live, then again with the phone's WebSocket refused (the 15 s fallback). The matrix's `e2e/live-viewer.spec.ts` shows the player view with `get_live_stack` stubbed.
